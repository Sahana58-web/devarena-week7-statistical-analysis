HYPOTHESIS TESTS (alpha = 0.05)

----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 1: Are West orders smaller than orders in the other regions? (Welch t-test)
  H0 (no effect):  mean order value is the same in West and the other regions
  H1 (effect):     mean order value is different
  Result:          West mean = 81,689, others mean = 138,394, t = -2.72, Cohen's d = -0.58, p-value = 0.008887
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): REJECT H0 (significant)
  Meaning:         West orders are about Rs.56,704 smaller on average. The non-parametric Mann-Whitney test agrees (p = 0.003335). West was chosen after seeing the data, so treat this as exploratory; even with a Bonferroni correction for the 4 regions (p x 4 = 0.036) it stays significant.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 2: Are Laptop orders bigger than orders for other products? (Welch t-test)
  H0 (no effect):  mean order value is the same for Laptop and other products
  H1 (effect):     mean order value is different
  Result:          Laptop mean = 162,050, others mean = 111,524, t = 1.94, Cohen's d = 0.51, p-value = 0.06093
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): FAIL TO REJECT H0 (not significant)
  Meaning:         Laptop orders look bigger, but with only 24 laptop orders the data cannot rule out chance at the 5% level.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 3: Does the mean order value differ between the 5 products? (one-way ANOVA)
  H0 (no effect):  all 5 products have the same mean order value
  H1 (effect):     at least one product has a different mean
  Result:          F = 2.05, p-value = 0.09343
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): FAIL TO REJECT H0 (not significant)
  Meaning:         No clear evidence of a difference between products. The Kruskal-Wallis test agrees (p = 0.1082).


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 4: Does the mean order value differ between the 4 regions? (one-way ANOVA)
  H0 (no effect):  all 4 regions have the same mean order value
  H1 (effect):     at least one region has a different mean
  Result:          F = 2.16, p-value = 0.09724
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): FAIL TO REJECT H0 (not significant)
  Meaning:         Overall, the 4 regions are not clearly different at the 5% level, even though West on its own looked smaller in Test 1.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 5: Is leaving (churn) linked with the contract type? (chi-square test)
  H0 (no effect):  churn is independent of the contract type
  H1 (effect):     churn depends on the contract type
  Result:          chi-square = 27.72, degrees of freedom = 2, p-value = 9.587e-07
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): REJECT H0 (significant)
  Meaning:         Churn rates: month-to-month 20.6%, one year 4.3%, two year 6.9%. Month-to-month customers leave much more often.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 6: Do customers who left have a different tenure? (Welch t-test)
  H0 (no effect):  mean tenure is the same for customers who left and who stayed
  H1 (effect):     mean tenure is different
  Result:          left mean = 6.0 months, stayed mean = 40.2 months, t = -34.53, Cohen's d = -1.92, p-value = 8.982e-130
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): REJECT H0 (significant)
  Meaning:         Customers who left had only a few months of tenure. The difference is huge and not due to chance.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 7: Do customers who left pay different monthly charges? (Welch t-test)
  H0 (no effect):  mean monthly charge is the same for customers who left and who stayed
  H1 (effect):     mean monthly charge is different
  Result:          left mean = 129.8, stayed mean = 111.7, t = 2.65, Cohen's d = 0.35, p-value = 0.01008
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): REJECT H0 (significant)
  Meaning:         Customers who left paid more per month on average, a small to moderate difference.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 8: Is churn linked with PaymentMethod? (chi-square test)
  H0 (no effect):  churn is independent of PaymentMethod
  H1 (effect):     churn depends on PaymentMethod
  Result:          chi-square = 3.87, degrees of freedom = 2, p-value = 0.1445
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): FAIL TO REJECT H0 (not significant)
  Meaning:         No evidence of a link.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 9: Is churn linked with PaperlessBilling? (chi-square test)
  H0 (no effect):  churn is independent of PaperlessBilling
  H1 (effect):     churn depends on PaperlessBilling
  Result:          chi-square = 0.05, degrees of freedom = 1, p-value = 0.8292
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): FAIL TO REJECT H0 (not significant)
  Meaning:         No evidence of a link.


----------------------------------------------------------------------

TEST 10: Is churn linked with SeniorCitizen? (chi-square test)
  H0 (no effect):  churn is independent of SeniorCitizen
  H1 (effect):     churn depends on SeniorCitizen
  Result:          chi-square = 0.07, degrees of freedom = 1, p-value = 0.7951
  Decision (alpha = 0.05): FAIL TO REJECT H0 (not significant)
  Meaning:         No evidence of a link.

  Test 1: West vs other regions (order value): p = 0.00889, adjusted p = 0.0889 -> NOT significant after the correction
  Test 2: Laptop vs other products (order value): p = 0.0609, adjusted p = 0.609 -> NOT significant after the correction
  Test 3: ANOVA across products: p = 0.0934, adjusted p = 0.934 -> NOT significant after the correction
  Test 4: ANOVA across regions: p = 0.0972, adjusted p = 0.972 -> NOT significant after the correction
  Test 5: Churn vs contract type: p = 9.59e-07, adjusted p = 9.59e-06 -> still significant
